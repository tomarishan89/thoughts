### 5.2 Theoretical & Mechanical Deconstruction

#### 1. Altman Z-Score: The Intangible Sclerosis & Inverted Odds Ratio Failure

Altman's discriminant function relies on physical working capital and accumulated retained earnings:

$$Z \equiv 1.2 \frac{\text{Working Capital}}{\text{Assets}} + 1.4 \frac{\text{Retained Earnings}}{\text{Assets}} + 3.3 \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Assets}} + 0.6 \frac{M_F}{\text{Total Debt}} + 0.999 \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Assets}}$$

In the 55-firm modern economy, Altman Z achieves an Odds Ratio of only $0.80$ and precision of $29.7\%$ ( below the $32.5\%$ base rate ). 
The mathematical failure is structural:
- **Intangible Asset Misclassification:** Modern high-performing platform monopolies ( Microsoft, Apple, Broadcom ) execute aggressive share repurchases and operate with negative physical working capital ( suppliers finance inventory ). Altman penalizes these engines, falsely categorizing them in the "distress" or "grey" zones.
- **Inverted Directionality:** Because modern market leaders have low physical book working capital, Altman flags them as distressed while they Accrete value, causing Altman's distress signal to exhibit an inverted correlation with forward equity drawdowns.

#### 2. Merton Distance-to-Default: The Equity Bubble Blindspot

Merton models equity as a call option on total assets $V_A = M_F + D$ under geometric Brownian motion:

$$\text{DD} \equiv \frac{\ln(V_A / D) + (r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma_A^2)T}{\sigma_A \sqrt{T}}$$

In the empirical benchmark, Merton Distance-to-Default achieved $0.0\%$ precision and $0.0\%$ recall:
- **Tautological Equity Cushion:** Because Merton treats equity market capitalization $M_F$ as a direct asset cushion, a corporation undergoing a massive parasitic equity bubble ( e.g., Boeing distributing $\$40\text{B}$ in debt-financed buybacks while engineering capacity eroded ) displays an artificially massive distance-to-default ( $\text{DD} > 4.5\sigma$ ).
- **Inability to Detect Substrate Hollowing:** Merton cannot distinguish between organic asset accretion and phantom market cap inflation. To Merton, higher stock prices *guarantee* credit safety. The Corporate Open Engine framework solves this by subtracting substrate productivity, recognizing that $dM_F/d\tau \gg dM_{\text{sub}}/d\tau$ is the definition of parasitic vulnerability.

The Fama-French 5-factor model represents empirical cross-sectional regressions:

$$R_{it} - R_{ft} = \alpha_i + \beta_1 \text{MKT}_t + \beta_2 \text{SMB}_t + \beta_3 \text{HML}_t + \beta_4 \text{RMW}_t + \beta_5 \text{CMA}_t + \epsilon_{it}$$
