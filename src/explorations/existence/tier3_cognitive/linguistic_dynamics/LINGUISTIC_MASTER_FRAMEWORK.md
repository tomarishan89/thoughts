## Executive Abstract

This sub-domain framework formalizes internal linguistic dialogue ( e.g., silent self-talk in English ) as a discrete symbolic open sub-engine $E_{\text{lang}} \subset \Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$. We address the empirical phenomenon where an agent employs motivational self-dialogue, triggering an autocatalytic surge of imaginary anisotropy ( a "ferocious motivational state" ), which subsequently decouples from real-world motor output and creates severe energetic depletion and cognitive friction when attempting to resume physical work.

Four master continuum equations govern linguistic dynamics:
1. **The Lexical Projection Algebra:** Discrete lexical operators $\hat{w}_k$ deforming the internal potential curvature of $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$.
2. **The Autocatalytic Anisotropy Rate Law:** Coupled evolution equation balancing linguistic gain $\alpha_{\text{lang}}$ against somatic motor discharge $\Gamma_{\text{discharge}} \mathbf{J}_{\text{motor}}$.
3. **The Parasitic Runaway Criterion:** Mathematical condition for ungrounded exponential accumulation of imaginary anisotropy leading to metabolic exhaustion.
4. **The Return-to-Work Potential Barrier:** Continuum formulation of the steep energetic gradient separating hyper-inflated imaginary aspirations from stagnant somatic reality.

## 1. Master Continuum Equations of Linguistic Dynamics

### Master Equation LD-1: The Lexical Projection Algebra

Words and syntactic structures in internal speech act as discrete projection operators $\hat{w}_k \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{lang}}$ on the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{Im}}$, altering the local curvature of the internal representation landscape $\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{Im}}$:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{Im}}^{(n+1)}(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{Im}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{z}) + \sum_k c_k \, \hat{w}_k^\dagger \, \mathbf{K}_{\text{trans}} \, \hat{w}_k}$$

Each discrete linguistic utterance injects localized directional curvature, restructuring the agent's perceived values, threats, and urgency.

---

### Master Equation LD-2: The Autocatalytic Rate Law

The time evolution of internal linguistic anisotropy $\mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ follows a coupled excitation-discharge differential equation:

$$\boxed{\frac{d\mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}}}{dt} = \alpha_{\text{lang}} \mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}} - \Gamma_{\text{discharge}} \mathbf{J}_{\text{motor}} \quad \left[ \frac{\mathrm{anisotropy}}{\mathrm{s}} \right]}$$

where $\alpha_{\text{lang}} > 0$ is the linguistic autocatalytic amplification coefficient, and $\Gamma_{\text{discharge}}$ governs the rate at which internal symbolic drive is transduced into physical somatic work $\mathbf{J}_{\text{motor}} \in \Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$.

---

### Master Equation LD-3: The Parasitic Runaway Limit

If somatic motor discharge is zero ( $\mathbf{J}_{\text{motor}} = \mathbf{0}$ )—meaning the agent engages in self-motivating monologue while physically pacing, roaming, or remaining stationary—the system decouples into an ungrounded exponential mode:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}}(t) = \mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}}(0) \exp\left( \alpha_{\text{lang}} t \right)}$$

The internal landscape acquires massive fictitious momentum ( the "ferocious motivational state" ). Because no real boundary work is executed in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{shared}}$, the physical substrate expends metabolic glucose without resolving any external challenge.

---

### Master Equation LD-4: The Return-to-Work Potential Barrier

When the linguistic loop ceases, the agent confronts the enlarged disparity between the elevated imaginary template $\mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ and the stagnant manifest state $\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{R}}$. The **Return-to-Work Barrier** is given by:

$$\boxed{\Delta V_{\text{work}} = \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{Im}}(t_{\text{peak}}) - \mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{R}}\|_{G_{\mathbb{C}}}^2 \gg \Delta V_{\text{baseline}}}$$

Resuming mundane physical labor requires surmounting this steep potential hill, manifesting phenomenologically as cognitive resistance, procrastination, fatigue, and frustration.
