## 0. The Error

The previous note (`generative_sequence_stimulus_to_form.md`) ran the entire generative sequence — stimulus → iteration → ledger → fronts → form — exclusively in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$. Every front $f_k$ was a spatial boundary. Every stimulus was a real-space traction. Every ledger entry was a real-space record.

This is wrong. The framework defines the state space as complexified:

$$\Omega_{\mathbb{C}} = \Omega_{\mathbb{R}} \oplus i\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$$

with BOTH sectors carrying physical content. The generative sequence must operate across the FULL complexified manifold, not just its real projection.

## 1. What the Framework Actually Says About $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$

From [draft.md §1.1](../core_ontology/draft.md#L45-L53):

**At Tier 1:** $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ is NOT empty. But these projections are **not self-referential**: they do not fold back to model the source.

**At Tier 2+:** $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ becomes **self-referential** — "forming a ledger-accumulated cognitive simulation space hosting multiple sub-entities."

The tier-defining transition is not just about complexity in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$. It is about the **topology of $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$** — whether the imaginary sector folds back on itself.

## 3. The Corrected Generative Sequence: Dual-Sector Feedback

The generative sequence operates on the FULL complexified manifold. Each element must be decomposed into its real and imaginary components:

### 3.1 Fronts in Both Spaces

Fronts are zero-level sets $f_k(t) = \{x \mid \phi_k(x, t) = 0\}$. But "x" lives in $\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}$, not just $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$.

**Real-space fronts** $f_k^{\mathbb{R}}$: Physical boundaries. Cell membrane, skin, event horizon, national border.

**Imaginary-space fronts** $f_k^{\mathfrak{Im}}$: Informational/cognitive boundaries. The self/other distinction, the known/unknown boundary, the safe/threatening classification, the legal/illegal frontier.

| Tier | Fronts in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$ | Fronts in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Photospheric surface, core-envelope interface, convective/radiative boundary | **None** (gauge fields propagate outward but create no self-referential boundary) |

**The tier transition is NOT just more fronts quantitatively. It is fronts APPEARING IN $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ for the first time.**

**Key observation:** The Tier 1 → Tier 2 transition is precisely the moment when the ledger BEGINS RECORDING IN $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$. It is a model of the entity stored within the entity. This is self-reference, and it is the defining property of $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ at Tier 2+.

### 3.3 Stimuli in Both Spaces

The stimulus-response channels also bifurcate:

**Real-space stimuli** → mechanical coupling (tidal force, pressure, radiation pressure, chemical concentration gradient).

**Imaginary-space stimuli** → informational coupling (a signal that is too weak to mechanically deform the boundary but carries structured information that modifies the entity's internal model in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ ).

At Tier 1: ALL stimuli are real-space. The star has no imaginary-space receptors. Its electromagnetic field propagates into $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ (the field IS in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ ) but the star does not READ its own field as information.

At Tier 2: The cell begins to process Regime 1 stimuli (signals below yield threshold) as information in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$. A photon of light is mechanically negligible to the cell — but the cell's photoreceptor protein converts it into a conformational change, which triggers a signaling cascade, which modifies gene expression. The photon's INFORMATION content is transduced into $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$.

At Tier 3: The imaginary-space stimulus channel dominates. A social insult has zero mechanical force — it does not deform the skin, does not change body temperature, does not alter osmotic pressure. But it massively deforms the ego-identity schema in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$, triggering real-space physiological responses (cortisol, sympathetic activation) via the Semantic Transduction Tensor $\mathbf{K}_{\text{trans}}$.

## 5. What This Changes About the Stimulus-Response Theorem

The Stimulus-Response Minimum Theorem ("at least one channel exists") still holds. But the DUAL-SECTOR structure adds a refinement:

**Weak form (Tier 1):** At least one stimulus-response channel exists in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$.

**Intermediate form (Tier 2):** At least one stimulus-response channel exists in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$ AND at least one in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ (informational coupling — the entity processes Regime 1 signals).

**Strong form (Tier 3):** At least one stimulus-response channel exists where the stimulus in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ (a purely informational signal with zero mechanical force) produces a measurable response in $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$ (real physiological change). The cross-coupling is active.

This is a graduated spectrum, not a binary. And it's testable at each level:

| Form | Test |
|---|---|
| Weak (Tier 1) | Apply a mechanical perturbation, measure boundary deformation |
| Intermediate (Tier 2) | Apply a sub-yield-threshold signal (e.g., a specific wavelength of light), measure whether the entity processes it informationally (gene expression change, receptor conformational shift) — NOT just thermally |
| Strong (Tier 3) | Apply a purely symbolic stimulus (a word, a gesture, a social signal with zero mechanical content), measure whether it produces a measurable real-space physiological response (cortisol, heart rate, postural change) |

## 6. Self-Critique: What Remains Missing

1. **The Kähler metric couples the sectors.** The framework equips $\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}$ with a Hermitian/Kähler metric $h(X, Y) = g(X, Y) + i\omega(X, Y)$. The symplectic form $\omega$ couples the real and imaginary sectors. I have not yet used the Kähler structure — I've treated $\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}$ and $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ as independent sectors linked only by $\mathbf{K}_{\text{trans}}$ and Regime 1 routing. The symplectic form may impose additional constraints on how the sectors co-evolve.

2. **Viscosity in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$?** The iteration clock $\tau_{\text{visc}} = \ell^2/\nu$ was defined for real-space viscosity. Does $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ have its own viscosity — a resistance to informational state change? If so, does a separate iteration clock exist in the imaginary sector? The framework's Israel-Stewart relaxation is formulated for real-space stress. An analogous relaxation in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$ (resistance to belief change, resistance to model update) would be a substantive extension.

3. **The front velocity equation in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$?** The level-set front velocity $v_n = c \cdot v_{\text{classical}} / \sqrt{c^2 + v_{\text{classical}}^2} + \gamma_{\text{surface}} \kappa_{\text{geom}}$ was derived for real-space boundaries. If fronts exist in $\Omega_{\mathfrak{Im}}$, what is their velocity equation? What is the "speed of light" limit in informational space? What is the "surface tension" that smooths cognitive boundaries? These are open questions.
