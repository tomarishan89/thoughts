Consider a ubiquitous claim in contemporary discourse:

> **"I follow Sanatan Dharm."**

> *"I follow the total periodic table, quantum electrodynamics, and cosmic gravitation."*

In Sanskrit, **सनातन (Sanatan)** denotes that which is eternal, uncreated, continuous, and timeless. If **Sanatan Dharm** is the eternal, infinite matrix of all structural persistence laws across all forms of existence in spacetime, no localized entity can "follow" it. An entity merely instantiates a temporary, localized subset of those laws.

### Operational Distinction:

   * When exposed to high acoustic or kinetic energy, sensory factors detect the perturbation *before* mechanical yield occurs. ## Section 3: Mathematical Formalization: Vector Resistance Across Operational Fronts

### 3.1 Interacting Challenge Entities ($E^{\text{challenge}}$) and Impact Functions $\mathbf{I}_k(t)$
A challenge vector ($\mathcal{C}$) is not an abstract ghost; it is an interacting Form of Existence ($E^{\text{challenge}}$) deploying its own Dharm ($\mathcal{D}^{\text{challenge}}$) against a host entity ($E^{\text{host}}$).

$$\mathbf{I}_k(t) = \mathcal{D}^{\text{challenge}}(t) \quad \text{interrogating front } f_k \text{ of } E^{\text{host}}$$

The mathematical profile of $\mathbf{I}_k(t)$ takes three primary forms:
1. **Impulsive Load (Gaussian / Delta Function):** Acute, high-amplitude shocks over near-zero time ($\Delta t \to 0$).
2. **Cumulative Stress (Sigmoidal Function):** Asymptotic saturation of stress over prolonged durations.
3. **Resonant Vector ($\mathbf{I}(t) = A \sin(\omega_0 t)$):** If the challenge frequency matches the internal natural frequency ($\omega_0$) of host front $f_k$, internal stress amplifies exponentially:

$$\lim_{t \to \infty} \text{Internal Strain}(f_k) = \infty \quad \text{even if energy amplitude } A \text{ is small}$$

This explains why targeted, low-magnitude challenges matching a system's internal frequency (e.g., resonant acoustic waves shattering glass, or targeted subversion exploiting a specific socio-cultural rift) force systemic collapse without requiring massive raw force.

### 3.2 The Front Margin Delta ($\Delta \mathbf{R}$) and Dynamic Operational Extent
An entity does not face stress as a scalar uniform lump. It interacts along specific boundary interfaces called **Fronts ($f_k$)**.

Let $E_t^i$ denote a specific form of existence at time $t$.  
Let $\mathcal{C}_t^i(f_k)$ denote the Contest interrogating front $f_k$.  
Let $\mathbf{R}_t^i(f_k)$ denote the vector resistance offered by the entity's factor set on front $f_k$.  
Let $\mathbf{R}_{\text{min}}(f_k)$ denote the minimum required resistance threshold to prevent failure on front $f_k$.

We define the **Front Margin Delta ($\Delta \mathbf{R}$)**:

$$\Delta \mathbf{R}_t(f_k) = \mathbf{R}_{\text{offered}}(f_k) - \mathbf{R}_{\text{min}}(f_k)$$


The three dynamic operational trajectories are:
1. **Local Breach ($\Delta \mathbf{R}_t(f_k) < 0$):** Offered resistance is inadequate. Front $f_k$ suffers structural erosion, localized puncture, or functional degradation.
2. **Equilibrium ($\Delta \mathbf{R}_t(f_k) = 0$):** Offered resistance balances the impact function exactly. Boundary is statically maintained.
3. **Growth / Boundary Expansion ($\Delta \mathbf{R}_t(f_k) > 0$):** Deployed factor resistance exceeds the minimum survival threshold. In biological, cognitive, or organizational systems, surplus factor capacity pushes the operational perimeter outward into new state-space:

$$\text{Growth Vector: } \frac{d(\partial E_t)}{dt} \propto \sum_{k \in \text{Fronts}} \max\left(0, \; \Delta \mathbf{R}_t(f_k)\right)$$
